# Super Manifest – Research Program

## Scope

This document defines the **global epistemic assumptions**
of the research program.

It applies across:
- all domains
- all theories
- all paradigms
- all classifications
- all downstream systems

It is not a theory.
It is not a method.
It is not a framework.

It is a **boundary document**.

---

## Purpose

The purpose of this research program is not to explain the world.

Its purpose is to define
the conditions under which a **limited cognitive agent**
can meaningfully engage with an **overwhelming reality**.

The research program exists
because the world is larger than any mind.

---

## Preconditions of Research

Research is only possible if:

- the world is not fully representable
- cognition is not unlimited
- reduction is unavoidable
- loss cannot be eliminated
- responsibility must be localized

These are not hypotheses.
They are structural facts.

---

## Problems as Epistemic Cuts

Research begins with a cut.

This cut:
- separates relevant from irrelevant
- turns continuity into discreteness
- enables distinction
- makes responsibility possible

This cut is called a **problem**.

A problem is not a question.
It is an epistemic decision.

---

## Maximal Simplification

The formulation of a problem is a **maximal simplification**.

There is no further reduction possible
before the problem itself disappears.

All subsequent activity
(models, solutions, theories, calculations)
operates **within** this simplification.

The problem is the hardest epistemic boundary.

---

## Theories and Paradigms

Theories and paradigms do not stand above problems.

They:
- structure how problems can be formulated
- constrain what can be seen
- enable certain cuts and exclude others

In this sense,
theories are themselves
**high-level, historically stabilized problem solutions**.

They are inputs, not authorities.

---

## No Total Perspective

There is no:
- complete view
- neutral standpoint
- final theory
- global solution

Any claim to totality
is a denial of reduction
and therefore an epistemic error.

---

## Responsibility

Because every problem is a cut,
and every cut produces loss,
research requires responsibility.

Responsibility means:
- accepting the consequences of the cut
- acknowledging what is excluded
- not claiming more than the problem allows

Responsibility cannot be delegated
to theories, methods, or frameworks.

---

## Relation to Downstream Systems

This research program does not evaluate outcomes.

It provides:
- the epistemic ground
- the boundary conditions
- the minimal discipline

All concrete work happens downstream.

---

## Closure

This research program does not grow
by adding content.

It remains stable
as long as the asymmetry
between world and cognition remains.

As long as the world is large
and the mind is small,
this program applies.
